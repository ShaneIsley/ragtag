# Using Policy Desk

A short guide for UNSW staff, students and researchers. Policy Desk answers questions about how AI may be used at UNSW, from 17 of UNSW's public policies and guidance pages, and shows you the passage behind every statement.

It's an independent demonstration, not a UNSW service, and its answers aren't UNSW advice. It closes at the end of November 2026.

## Asking a question

Type your question and choose **Ask**. Good questions are about one thing, and say who you are when that matters:

- "I'm a tutor. Can I use AI to help mark assignments?"
- "Can I put student data into ChatGPT Edu?"
- "Do I need to acknowledge AI if I only used it to brainstorm?"
- "Who has to approve a new AI tool before I use it?"
- "How do I reference ChatGPT in APA?"

Name the tool if your question is about one: rules differ between Copilot, ChatGPT Edu, DeepSeek and others.

**Leave out personal details.** You never need a name, email address, phone number or zID to ask about the rules. The service removes those before your question goes anywhere and tells you what it removed, but detection can miss things, and health information isn't removed.

Each question is answered on its own: follow-up questions that rely on an earlier one aren't understood yet, so include what the follow-up is about.

## Reading the answer

- **The first sentence answers the question.** The details follow.
- **Numbers in brackets, like [2], are citations.** Each points to a passage in the **Sources** list below the answer. Open a source to read the passage itself and follow the link to UNSW's page.
- **Rule or guidance.** Answers say whether a point is something you must do (a rule) or something UNSW recommends (guidance), and whom it applies to.
- **How this answer was made.** Under the answer, closed until you open it: the question as it was sent (with anything removed), the broader question it searched for, every passage the model was given with how close each was to your question and which it cited, the model and prompt, and how long it took. The trace ID lets the operator look into a wrong answer.
- **Example questions,** below the question box, ask themselves when you choose one: some for staff, students and researchers, and one about another university that it should decline.
- **A content safety note** above the answer means Microsoft's filter rated your question as possibly harmful. It's shown so you know; it doesn't mean you did anything wrong.

**Check the source before you rely on an answer,** especially for anything with consequences: an answer can leave out a condition, and a few questions sometimes get a wrong detail (the service's status page lists them). For your course's rules, your course outline and convenor have the final word.

## When it doesn't answer

It declines instead of guessing, and says why:

| It says | What it means | What to try |
|---|---|---|
| "I couldn't find anything about that in the UNSW documents I cover." | Your question isn't close to anything in the sources | Check it's about AI at UNSW; name the tool or situation |
| "The UNSW documents I cover don't answer that." | The sources are about the topic but don't answer this question | Ask what UNSW requires, or check UNSW's pages directly |
| "I couldn't produce an answer backed by the policies." | An answer was written but couldn't be tied to a passage, so it was withheld | Rephrase the question |
| "Azure's content safety filter blocked this question." | Microsoft's filter blocked it | Rephrase; ask about the rules rather than describing harm |

## Trying the other model

**Try the other model** asks Claude Haiku 4.5 on Amazon Bedrock the same question, from the same kind of sources, and shows its answer below the first. It's a way to compare. The same model answers automatically if the main one is unavailable, and the page says so. It's checked weekly, and states fewer of the expected facts than the main model.

## Your privacy

Your question isn't stored, and refreshing the page clears it. Your IP address isn't recorded. The answer model may process your question outside Australia, which is why personal details are removed and why you should leave them out. The page's "How your question is handled" section has the detail.

## Light or dark, and black and white

The page follows your device's light or dark setting. To choose one yourself, use **Theme** at the bottom of the page: Auto, Light or Dark. **Colours** offers black and white for reading: higher contrast, a typeface designed to keep similar letters apart, slightly larger text, and every link underlined. Your browser remembers both choices; they aren't sent anywhere.

## How it's checked

Test questions with known right answers are asked of the live service every night and every week, and the AI judges that mark them are themselves checked against fixed answers. The **Service status** page, linked at the bottom of the main page, shows the latest results, known issues, and how many questions were answered, without any text.
